### A. Term life: suitable when there is a financial dependency

₹1 crore is approximately 13 times stated annual in-hand income. This is a useful preliminary benchmark, but **not a substitute for a needs-based cover calculation**.

> **Required life cover = dependant-support capital + liabilities + unfunded family goals − assets earmarked for those needs − suitable existing life cover.**

**CFP recommendation:**
- Proceed with pure term insurance if you have financial dependants or material obligations.
- If you have neither, assess whether purchasing term cover now is necessary rather than treating it as compulsory.
- Match the policy term to the expected duration of financial dependency.
- Review death-benefit options, exclusions, disclosure obligations, lapse provisions and any riders.
- Disclose health, smoking, occupation and other requested information accurately.

### B. Health insurance: prioritise independent protection

A ₹10 lakh policy is a reasonable **starting point for assessment** at age 26. Its adequacy depends on your city, medical history, employer coverage and preferred hospitals.

Before selecting the proposed policy, verify the exact variant’s:

- Room-rent restrictions, co-payments, deductibles and disease sub-limits.
- Pre-existing-condition and specified-treatment waiting periods.
- Exclusions and non-payable expenses.
- Cashless hospital network.
- Restoration conditions and how repeat claims are treated.

If higher protection is needed, compare a larger base policy with a suitably structured super top-up.

### C. Accident and disability: assess the missing protection

Neither ordinary term life cover nor hospitalisation insurance necessarily replaces income lost through disability. Review employer benefits and obtain comparable quotations for personal accident/disability protection.

**Funding rule:** The difference between the proposed premiums and the insurance ceiling is already allocated to investments. Any additional premium requires reducing investments or discretionary spending with approval. Escalate inadequate protection rather than silently exceeding the budget.

### CFP assessment of the allocation

The asset mix is approximately:

- **Equity: 76.9%**
- **Liquid debt: 14.2%**
- **Gold: 8.9%**

This is an **aggressive growth portfolio**, not a balanced-risk portfolio.

- **Core equity:** The flexi-cap and Nifty 50 funds provide diversified equity exposure, though their holdings may overlap.
- **Mid- and small-cap exposure:** Together they represent **26.6% of the total portfolio**, increasing volatility and potential drawdowns. Use only for goals generally **10 years or more away**.
- **Liquid fund:** Offers relatively low interest-rate sensitivity and liquidity, but carries risks and is not a return guarantee.
- **Gold:** Can diversify portfolio risks, but does not reliably hedge inflation over every period.

A risk score of 7/10 alone is insufficient to approve this allocation. Confirm that you can tolerate a severe, potentially **30% or greater temporary portfolio decline**, without needing to sell.

Money required within approximately three years should generally be held in capital-stable instruments outside the equity/gold allocation. If your capacity for loss is lower, reduce the mid-/small-cap allocation before implementation.

### Costs and maintenance

Check current expense ratios, exit loads, index tracking difference, ETF bid–ask spreads, brokerage, demat costs and applicable taxation. Direct plans avoid distributor commissions but still have expenses.

- Review cashflow **quarterly**.
- Review goals, insurance and allocation **annually**.
- Consider rebalancing when an asset class moves around **five percentage points** from its approved target.
- Prefer new contributions for rebalancing where practical.
- Reduce risk as goal dates approach.
- Include existing EPF and other holdings when assessing total household asset allocation.

## 5. Tax Strategy: New Regime Assessment and Conditional Zero Tax

### Corrections to the specialist calculation

The supplied tax summary does **not** establish your actual liability because:

1. It treats **in-hand salary as gross salary**.
2. Its ₹50,000 standard deduction does not match the **FY 2025–26 new-regime salary framework**.
3. It combines deduction, slab and rebate assumptions that do not support the stated computation consistently.

As this plan is dated October 2026, the relevant current financial year is **FY 2026–27 / AY 2027–28**. Current-year provisions must be checked before filing or changing withholding; they have not been independently verified here.

### Conditional illustration—not your payroll calculation

The following uses the **FY 2025–26 framework**, assuming an eligible resident individual with ₹7,68,000 **gross salary**, no other income and no special-rate income.

| Item | New-regime illustration | Old-regime illustration |
|---|---:|---:|
| Assumed gross salary | ₹7,68,000 | ₹7,68,000 |
| Standard deduction | ₹75,000 | ₹50,000 |
| Taxable income | ₹6,93,000 | ₹7,18,000 |
| Income tax before rebate and cess | ₹14,650 | ₹56,100 |
| Eligible rebate | ₹14,650 | ₹0 |
| Cess | ₹0 | ₹2,244 |
| **Illustrative annual liability** | **₹0** | **₹58,344** |

The old-regime example assumes **no additional deductions or exemptions**. Under the illustrated new-regime framework, qualifying ordinary income up to ₹12 lakh can receive rebate relief; special-rate income requires separate analysis.

### CFP conclusion on savings and zero tax

- **Illustrative regime saving:** ₹58,344 annually under the assumptions above.
- **Actual regime recommendation:** The new regime is a strong candidate, subject to current-year verification and comparison using actual payroll and deductions.
- **Actual zero-tax confirmation:** **Not yet possible from in-hand salary alone.**

Obtain gross salary, salary components, employer retirement contributions, TDS, residency details, other income and available deductions. Then complete the current-year comparison and adjust withholding if needed.

**Do not add the illustrative tax saving to this budget.** Your ₹64,000 in-hand salary already reflects payroll deductions. Do not buy insurance or locked-in investments solely to generate deductions.
