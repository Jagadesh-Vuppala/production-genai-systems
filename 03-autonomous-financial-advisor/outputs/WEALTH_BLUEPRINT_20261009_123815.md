### Health insurance: first priority

An individual health policy can provide continuity beyond employment, but the appropriate cover depends on location, medical history, family circumstances and existing benefits.

Before purchase, compare:
- Waiting periods and pre-existing disease provisions.
- Room-rent restrictions, co-payments and exclusions.
- Network hospitals, restoration conditions and renewal terms.
- Exact policy variant, underwriting terms and final payable premium.

**CFP assessment:** Rs.10 lakh is a reasonable starting point for evaluation, not proof that the health-protection need is fully met.

### Term life: conditional priority

Rs.1 crore may be appropriate **if someone depends on the client’s income**, but an income multiple alone is insufficient.

Estimate the requirement from:

**Outstanding obligations + dependant support + unfunded goals − assets available for those needs.**

- If there are no financial dependants or relevant obligations, term cover may not be an immediate priority.
- Confirm policy tenure, disclosures, exclusions and premium-payment structure.
- Prefer pure protection rather than investment-linked insurance.
- If term cover is deferred, redirect the unused provision to savings; do not treat it as permission for additional consumption.

### Accident and disability: unresolved protection gap

Assess employer benefits and personal accident/disability protection. A young earner may face substantial financial disruption from loss of earning capacity even without dependants.

**No separate premium has been budgeted for this protection.** Obtain quotes and flag any resulting budget conflict.

## 5. Tax Strategy

### CFP correction: zero tax is not yet confirmed

The specialist treated **Rs.7,68,000 of annual take-home pay as gross salary**. These are not interchangeable. Gross salary may be higher because take-home pay can already reflect EPF, TDS and other payroll deductions.

The supplied calculation also uses a **Rs.7 lakh rebate threshold associated with earlier rules** and does not identify the financial year. It should not be used as a current-year tax certificate.

### Clearly dated illustration—not the client’s final assessment

Under **FY 2025–26 / AY 2026–27** rules, assuming:
- Indian resident individual eligibility;
- **Gross salary**, not take-home income, of Rs.7,68,000;
- No other income or special-rate income; and
- No old-regime deductions beyond its standard deduction:

| Calculation | New regime | Old regime |
|---|---:|---:|
| Assumed gross salary | Rs.7,68,000 | Rs.7,68,000 |
| Standard deduction | Rs.75,000 | Rs.50,000 |
| Taxable income | Rs.6,93,000 | Rs.7,18,000 |
| Tax before rebate and cess | Rs.14,650 | Rs.56,100 |
| **Tax after applicable rebate and cess** | **Rs.0** | **Rs.58,344** |

**Illustrative new-regime saving: Rs.58,344 annually.** This is not verified savings for the client and must not be added to the take-home budget.

For the current **FY 2026–27 / AY 2027–28**, confirm the applicable enacted rules before finalizing the calculation.

### Required tax actions

Obtain:
- Salary breakup, recent payslips, Form 16 and employer tax computation.
- EPF details, bonuses, taxable benefits and other income.
- AIS/Form 26AS, interest and capital-gains records.
- Evidence of eligible HRA, insurance and other deductions.

Compare both eligible regimes using actual figures. Do not purchase tax-saving products merely to claim deductions; common deductions such as Sections 80C and 80D generally do not apply under the new regime. Adjust employer declarations and TDS only after verification.
